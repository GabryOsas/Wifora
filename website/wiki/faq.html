Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is Wifora free?

Completely. Open source under the MIT license: use it, study it, modify it, even in commercial products. If it's useful, a coffee on Buy Me a Coffee is always appreciated.

Does it need an Internet connection?

No. Everything happens on your local network. The Internet is needed once, to download the project and npm dependencies; afterwards it can be gone entirely.

Does it work away from home / over the Internet?

No by design: Wifora streams only on the LAN, with no tunnels or cloud, to guarantee minimum latency and total privacy. It's not an online streaming service.

How many devices can listen at once?

Default 5, configurable up to 32 (WIFORA_MAX_LISTENERS). In practice beyond 8–10 simultaneous streams the Wi-Fi access point is the limit, especially on 2.4 GHz.

Which phones are supported?

iPhone/iPad on iOS/iPadOS 15+, Android 10+ with Chrome, and any modern desktop as a secondary listener.

Does it consume mobile data?

No: traffic flows only between your PC and devices on the LAN, never to the Internet or through the mobile network.

What's the maximum audio quality?

Fullband Opus 48 kHz stereo: 256 kbps at the top adaptive tier, fixed 384 kbps with Hi-Fi. Same codec family as WhatsApp/YouTube Music — here without artificial caps.

Why not AirPlay or Chromecast?

Those protocols impose 1–2 s buffered latency and/or closed ecosystems. Wifora wants near-perfect sync on any device: WebRTC P2P is the most direct route. An RAOP/AirPlay prototype exists in the sources for the future.

Can I record what I broadcast?

Yes: the record button on the host saves a WebM/M4A file of the session, straight from the pre-codec signal.

Is my PC broadcasting even when nobody listens?

Audio is only captured and encoded after you press Start; with zero listeners there's no network traffic. Stop ends everything.

What data does Wifora collect?

None. No analytics, no cloud, no update checks. Logs stay in your terminal and contain no secrets.

Could a neighbor listen to my room?

They'd have to be on your LAN and know the full URL+token (128 bits). From outside your network they can't reach the server at all. With HTTPS enabled not even metadata is readable on the wire.

Does it work with Spotify, games, Zoom…?

Yes: what gets streamed is the system mix of the active Windows endpoint — whatever app is playing. The "Voice" DSP mode enhances dialogue and calls.

Browser capture vs native WASAPI: any difference?

Listeners get the same Opus stream either way; the WASAPI path (optional helper) is often more robust and independent of browser versions. See the native helper page.

Why 20 ms framing?

It's the sweet spot on Wi-Fi: packets big enough not to saturate RF airtime, small enough to keep encoding latency under 25 ms. The gaming profile drops to 10 ms CBR.

Why no STUN/TURN?

STUN/TURN servers exist to traverse NAT toward the Internet: pointless and harmful on a LAN (extra latency, lost privacy). With iceServers: [] only local candidates are used.

Which languages are available?

The app interface ships in Italian and English (selector top-right, browser auto-detection). This website exists in both languages too.

Can I contribute or fork it?

Absolutely: MIT allows it explicitly. Start from the Development & Contributing page.
